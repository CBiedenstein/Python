Physics Context: In 3D stacked-beam radars (TPS-43/70/75), strong surface clutter ($\sigma_c$) enters the lowest elevation beam ($B_1$). Due to finite antenna isolation ($I_{ant}$), this energy leaks into upper beams ($B_2 \dots B_6$) via sidelobes, creating "Ghost Targets". The VCC Algorithm compares beam amplitudes to distinguish real high-altitude targets from this leakage.

Sidelobe Leakage Equation

$$ P_{leak}(i) = P_{main} - I_{iso} + \delta_{stoch} $$

Where $P_{main}$ is the power in Beam 1 (clutter source), $I_{iso}$ is the antenna isolation (usually 20-30dB), and $\delta_{stoch}$ represents statistical fluctuations (scintillation) causing "spiky" ghosts.

VCC Decision Equation

$$ \text{If } P_{rx}(i) < \left( P_{rx}(1) - I_{iso} + M_{safety} \right) \implies \text{Ghost} $$

The algorithm sets a dynamic threshold based on Beam 1. $M_{safety}$ is the safety margin (determined by "Mode"). If upper beam power is below this threshold, it is correlated with Beam 1 and blanked.
