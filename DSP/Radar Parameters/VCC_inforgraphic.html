● Phase 1: The IF Signal Processor (ISP)

Foundational linearity, dynamic range, and matched filtering.

The core of the NGSP is the **Digital IF Architecture**. By sampling at an intermediate frequency (IF) directly, we bypass analog mixers, eliminating DC offset and LO leakage issues. The critical research focus here is **Linearity** and **Pulse Compression**.

Key Physics Equation: Matched Filter

The output $y[n]$ maximizes SNR by convolving with the time-reversed conjugate of the transmit pulse:

$$ y[n] = \sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} x[k] h^*[n-k] $$
  • ✓ Taylor Weighting: Applied to suppress time-domain sidelobes below -30dB.
  • ✓ Dynamic Range: ADC dithering ensures SFDR > 80dB.

● Phase 2: Interference Suppression

Handling asynchronous pulses and environmental noise pre-MTD.

Fig 2. IQ Constellation showing coherent signal (tight cluster) vs. asynchronous interference (scattered points).

Before Doppler processing, the system must clean the spectrum. Asynchronous Interference (pulses from other radars) manifests as random noise spikes in the time domain or scattered points in the IQ plane.

Suppression Strategy

  • PRN Jitter: Varying PRI to decorrelate interference.
  • Side Lobe Blanking (SLB): Comparing Omni vs Main channel gain.

● Phase 3: Vertical Clutter Cancellation (VCC)

Solving the "Punch Through" problem in Stacked Beam Radars (TPS-75).

The "Ghost Target" Phenomenon

In 3D radars, strong ground clutter enters the lowest beam ($B_1$). Due to finite antenna isolation ($\sim 25dB$), this energy leaks into upper elevation beams ($B_2 \dots B_6$), appearing as high-altitude "Ghost" targets.

Fig 3. Energy distribution across elevation beams. Orange represents clutter leakage ("Punch Through").

VCC Threshold Logic

A target in Beam $i$ is declared a "Ghost" if its power $P_i$ correlates with Beam 1 power $P_1$ minus isolation $I_{iso}$:

$$ P_{i} < (P_{1} - I_{iso} + \Delta_{margin}) \rightarrow \text{Reject} $$
